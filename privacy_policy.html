Privacy Policy for SwipeLock

Last updated: October 24, 2026

Summary

SwipeLock shows one of your photos on screen and pins it there, so you can hand your phone to someone without them browsing the rest of your gallery.

  • Your photos and videos never leave your device. We do not upload, copy or store them.
  • There is no sign-in and no account. We never ask for or receive your name, email address or phone number.
  • The app is free with no purchases and no subscriptions.
  • We do collect diagnostic and device information, described below.
  • The app shows no ads. From version 1.2.1 it does tell Meta when it has been installed and opened, using your Android advertising ID, so that we can measure our own ads for SwipeLock. You can switch this off, and on phones the app can tell are in the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland it starts switched off.

Your Photos and Videos

The app needs permission to read the media on your device so it can show it to you. That reading happens entirely on your phone. Your photos and videos are never transmitted to us or to anyone else.

The app can also delete a photo or share it to another app, but only when you explicitly ask it to. Sharing hands the file to the app you pick, which then handles it under its own privacy policy.

What We Collect

All of the following is handled by Google Firebase on our behalf, except the ad measurement report, which goes to Meta.

  • An anonymous account identifier. On first launch the app creates a random, anonymous Firebase account. It is not linked to any real-world identity. Firebase also records the IP address the request came from.
  • Installation details. The date you installed the app and the app version.
  • Device information. Manufacturer, brand, model, Android version, security patch level, supported CPU types, build identifiers and whether the device is physical or emulated, and whether the app is a test build.
  • An installation identifier (Firebase Installation ID), used to tell one install of the app apart from another.
  • Crash and error reports (Firebase Crashlytics), including device state at the time of the crash, so we can fix faults.
  • Basic usage analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase): an app-instance identifier, your device language and country, whether the device is an emulator or runs a test build of the app, an event recorded when you lock a photo (with how many items you locked), and an event recorded when you share the app, with the name of the app you shared it through (for example WhatsApp). It also records your answer when the app offers reminders and, if you turn them on, when a reminder is shown or opened. If you installed SwipeLock from a link someone shared from the app, Analytics also records that the install came from a share. Analytics derives an approximate location from your IP address. We do not record which photo you locked, or who you shared the app with.
  • Configuration requests (Firebase Remote Config), which include your country, language, timezone, OS version and app version. If you turn on reminders, the app also makes this request in the background in the evening, to fetch the reminder texts. Reminders are created on your phone; no push service is used.
  • The Android advertising ID, from version 1.2.1. This is the resettable identifier that Google Play services gives each device. Google Analytics for Firebase reads it, and so does the Meta SDK described next.
  • Ad measurement (Meta SDK), from version 1.2.1. We advertise SwipeLock on Facebook and Instagram. So that we can tell which of those ads lead to installs, the app tells Meta Platforms when it has been installed, when it is opened and how long it stayed open. Those reports carry the advertising ID, a random identifier that the Meta SDK creates for this install of the app, the Google Play install referrer, which app store installed the app, on older Android versions an attribution identifier supplied by the Facebook app if it is installed, and basic device and app details such as model, Android version, language, timezone, mobile carrier, screen size, storage size and app version. Meta also sees the IP address the reports came from, and receives reports of errors inside its own code and of changes to these settings. We do not tell Meta which photos you view or lock, or what you do inside the app.

The app shows no ads and includes no ad network. The Meta SDK is there only to measure installs that come from our own ads for SwipeLock.

We do not collect precise location, contacts, messages, call logs, or the contents of your photos.

Why We Collect It

To keep the app working and to understand how many people use it and roughly where, so we can decide what to build next. And, from version 1.2.1, to learn which of our own ads for SwipeLock lead to installs, so that those ads reach people who are likely to want the app.

We do not sell your data, and we do not build a profile of you. Meta uses the reports it receives to match installs to our ads and to decide who is shown them; what else Meta does with them is governed by Meta's own privacy policy, linked below.

Is This Data Anonymous?

We think it is fair to call it pseudonymous rather than anonymous. We never learn your name or email, but the data is tied to a persistent random identifier and includes detailed device characteristics. We do not attempt to identify individuals, and we have no practical means to do so.

The advertising ID is different. Google and Meta may be able to connect it to an account you hold with them. That is why it has its own switch, described under Your Choices.

Who We Share It With

Only the companies below. Google processes it for us. Meta receives the ad measurement report and, as described above, also uses it for its own purposes.

  • Google Firebase (Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Crashlytics, Analytics, Installations, Remote Config) — see Firebase's privacy policy.
  • Google Play — each time the app opens it asks Google Play whether a newer version is available. To answer, Play receives the app's version and basic device information. See Google's privacy policy.
  • Meta Platforms (Meta SDK), from version 1.2.1 — receives the ad measurement report described above, including the advertising ID. See Meta's privacy policy.

We do not sell or rent your data. Apart from the ad measurement report to Meta, we do not share it with any advertising company.

Retention and Deletion

Uninstalling the app does not delete the data already stored with Firebase. The anonymous account record and its device information remain until we delete them.

How long we keep it: an install record is kept for as long as the app is published and that install may still be in use — we have no fixed expiry date — or until you ask us to delete it, whichever comes first. Analytics and crash data expire on Google's own retention schedule, which we do not control. What Meta has received is kept under Meta's own rules, which we do not control either.

To have it deleted, email us at the address below. Because the account is anonymous, we cannot look you up by name — tell us roughly when you installed the app and on what device, and we will delete what we can identify within 30 days.

Your Choices

You can revoke the app's access to your photos at any time in Android Settings, and you can uninstall the app.

You can turn off usage analytics and ad measurement at any time in the app: Settings → Share usage data. It is one switch for both. Turning it off stops new analytics events and new reports to Meta, and neither Google Analytics nor Meta is given your advertising ID after that. A request that was already under way may still finish, and until the app is closed Meta's code may still fetch its own configuration, which shows Meta your IP address but carries none of the identifiers above. From the next time the app starts, the Meta SDK is not started at all. It does not delete what has already been collected — use the deletion request above for what we hold, and Meta's own tools for what Meta holds. Crash reports are still sent to Firebase, so that we can fix faults that affect you.

On phones the app can tell are in the European Union, the rest of the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, this switch starts off. The app judges that from your phone's region setting, your SIM and the mobile network you are on, and it also starts off when it cannot tell where the phone is. It is a judgement, not a certainty: a phone that shows none of those signs is treated as being elsewhere. Where the switch starts off, the usage analytics and ad measurement described above begin only if you switch them on. Everywhere else the switch starts on.

You can also reset or delete your advertising ID for every app in Android Settings, under Privacy or Google → Ads. The exact place depends on your phone. That does not change the identifier the Meta SDK created for this install; switching Share usage data off stops it being sent.

Advertising

The app shows no ads and includes no ad network. We do advertise SwipeLock itself on Facebook and Instagram, and from version 1.2.1 the app includes the Meta SDK only to measure installs from those ads, as described above.

We still intend to show advertising inside the app in a future version, chosen and served by us rather than by an ad network. If and when that happens we will update this policy before the feature ships.

Children

SwipeLock is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Security

All data sent to Firebase and to Meta is encrypted in transit using HTTPS. Access rules restrict each anonymous account's records to that account.
